## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 The question this report answers

**What is the smallest robust economic model that enables meaningful national development, different economic identities, resource interdependence, fair player trading, and long-term strategic decisions in Project X — without excessive complexity, runaway wealth, market abuse, or unreasonable hosting costs?**

### 1.2 The proposed economy in one paragraph (all provisional)

Project X's recommended economy is a **single-currency, five-development-line, four-material-class, directed-trade-first economy**: one national treasury (SYS-002) fed almost exclusively by productive income (SYS-003) and by player-to-player transfers (SYS-005); five abstract development lines (Production, Commerce, Administration, Works, Readiness) whose *levels* express national strength and whose *emphasis weights* (MO-001) express identity; four tradeable material classes (MAT-01…04) produced by emphasized lines and consumed by upkeep, investment, and (at the war-design stage) conflict; interdependence generated by **efficiency asymmetry** (buying a specialized nation's surplus is cheaper than underskilled self-production, SDQ-009) rather than by geography or artificial exclusions; directed bilateral trades as the first-playable exchange (SIM-008's shape), with a public listing market held behind a defined integrity/activity/write-budget gate; emphasis changes that are paid, queued-to-boundary, distance-gated, and subject to a bounded retooling dip (SDQ-016); and a fiscal model with no player debt, growing upkeep drains, and austerity-style floor rules for failure (SDQ-007's family).

## 3. Established constraints (restated; unchanged)

Restated verbatim-in-substance from [AI_CONTEXT.md](../AI_CONTEXT.md) §2 and the task brief; these are binding on the economy design:

1. **Game identity:** persistent, multiplayer, browser-based nation-strategy game in a fictional world combining development, economy, specialization, governance, diplomacy, trade, alliances, and abstract strategic conflict. Experience goal: *governing a nation in a living international community* — not operating a production spreadsheet.
2. **No geographic world map:** no borders, coordinates, territory, or geographic deposits. Spatially independent interaction reasons are a *hard* requirement; every economic mechanic below is map-tested.
3. **Shared turn cadence:** one global turn every two hours, twelve per day, fixed UTC boundaries (SYS-016 / PXO-003's three-class timing contract). No mechanic may require online presence at a boundary or continuous timers.
4. **Low-cost, AI-assisted, free-first development:** complexity must justify its storage, maintenance, and abuse surface. Cloudflare Workers + D1 remain *candidates* (D-005 Provisional).
5. **Original mechanics:** competitor patterns inform design; their distinctive mechanics, formulas, names, and balance values are not copied (AP-008).
6. **No approvals by implication:** nothing here is approved by being recommended; research-stage statuses only (D-003, Prompt 5 §18's rules-family, D-006 for FO items).

## 5. Economic design goals and success criteria (Deliverable 1)

### 5.1 The twelve goals (from the task), each resolved to a design stance

For each goal: **stance** (what the economy commits to), **applies-to mapping** (pillar/anti-pillar/frustration/persona/opportunity/system IDs), **evidence vs judgment**, and **success criterion** — with a measurable threshold where one can be justified by existing evidence, and a named experiment (ECV-/CV-/V-family) where it cannot.

**EG-1. Meaningful decisions without constant attendance.**
*Stance:* every economic decision is either immediate-with-record or queued-until-boundary (§14); nothing rewards being online *at* a boundary; drains resolve automatically with digest explanation.
*Maps:* DP-002, AP-001, F-004/F-019, Persona Mira (primary), FO-015, SYS-016/017.
*Evidence:* DP-002's session evidence is multi-source (Strong); *Judgment:* which specific decisions qualify is ours.
*Success criterion:* a player who plays 2 sessions/week loses *nothing* to absence beyond what time passing costs any idle nation — verified by the ECV-002 absence-equivalence walkthrough (§22.5) at paper stage, and by V-3's beta prototype. No attendance multiplier exists anywhere in the economy (checkable by design audit — no such mechanic need be listed in the timing table §14).

**EG-2. Multiple viable national development paths.**
*Stance:* the five development lines are deliberately unequal in opportunity cost (§7); no threshold grants a universal prerequisite; standing is plural (MO-007).
*Maps:* DP-001, AP-005, F-020, Persona Yuna (future tier) + Mira, FO-013, SDQ-001/009, MO-001/002.
*Evidence:* F-020's pain is cross-game (Strong); *Judgment:* the specific path set.
*Success criterion:* CV-003's plural-paths perception test passes when run (its existing hypothesis, unchanged); *additionally* — a paper-level test shown here: §18's "path-diversity scan" requires that for each development line, the model contains at least one worked scenario where *not* investing in it is the rational choice (§17A/B,D each demonstrate one). Falsifying repetition: if two lines' threshold effects are interchangeable in paper analysis, they are merged (SDQ-001 discipline).

**EG-3. Legible cause and effect for gains and losses.**
*Stance:* the digest explains every treasury/material delta with line-item provenance (§15); the finance view separates current state from forecast (forecast never presented as guaranteed); every failed order explains itself.
*Maps:* DP-004, AP-006, F-010/F-022, Persona Mira/Ade, FO-002/016, SYS-017/MO-003.
*Evidence:* F-010 multi-game churn (Strong); *Judgment:* provenance granularity.
*Success criterion:* CV-006 digest mock test (existing) + ECV-001: on the finance view, a reviewer can correctly reconstruct *why* last turn's balance changed from digest lines alone, no wiki — paper-tested in §17F, mock-tested at concept stage.

**EG-4. Real reasons to trade with other nations.**
*Stance:* interdependence comes from efficiency asymmetry and threshold structure (§9), not from artificial trade-only bonuses or mandatory transactions (DP-005 bounded by DP-001 per Prompt 5's interlock note).
*Maps:* DP-005, FO-013, SYS-004/005, MO-002, SDQ-009.
*Evidence:* FO-013's analysis + Prompt 1 §7.2's mapless gap (verified pattern-space, unverified design); *Judgment:* the asymmetry parameters (unresolved ECOQ-004).
*Success criterion:* the two-route sourcing test (§9.4) passes for all four material classes at design time; and ECV-001's two-nation paper scenario shows each nation's optimal plan includes at least one recurring purchase it cannot cheaply self-supply (§17A is the first instance; §18.2 extends it).

**EG-5. Specialization benefits without permanent build traps.**
*Stance:* emphasis changes exist, are paid, queued, distance-gated, and produce a bounded retooling dip (§11); no line-permanent locks; all lines technically always available (DP-001's no-unplayable-paths rule).
*Maps:* DP-001/DP-005, FO-013's slow-respecialization premise, SDQ-016, MO-001, F-012.
*Evidence:* FO-013's design premise (FV-lite pattern) + F-012's symmetric warning; *Judgment:* the three-part cost combination.
*Success criterion:* ECV-005's respecialization scenario check (paper, §17B/D) — a nation that chose a weak early emphasis can reach parity with an alternative build within a modeled bounded arc (symbolically, `retool_cost < advantage_lost_permanently`), and Stage E of the task's failure-mode pair ("too flexible"/"too restrictive") is explicitly scored in §11.5.

**EG-6. More than one source of economic success.**
*Stance:* success channels are divisible: production income, trade margins via specialization differentials, readiness-dependent conflict economics (deferred to war stage), and standing-through-reliability (trade-credit; SDQ-015's register). No single channel dominates by construction: threshold options, fees-influence-free markets, and plural registers keep wealth necessary-but-not-sufficient (DP-001; F-020).
*Maps:* DP-001, AP-005, MO-007's standing registers, FO-013, Persona Kwame (future tier).
*Evidence:* F-020 (Strong) — but note the model *cannot prove* the absence of dominance without simulation: recorded honestly (§14, §18). *Judgment:* the actual path list.
*Success criterion:* §18's dominance-scan finds no single economic lever whose paper-optimal strategy is "maximize this one lever"; ECOQ-007 keeps a playtest-era dominance review as a gate.

**EG-7. Manageable inflation and compounding.**
*Stance:* only productive income (and designated event-scale adjustments) creates currency; drains grow with levels; investment rewards are level-capped, never multiplicative over levels (no compound passive returns anywhere); §12's accounting framework separates creation/destruction/transfer.
*Maps:* F-004/F-019/FO-014; SIM-001's posture; CCR-008's level of ambition.
*Evidence:* Prompt 1 §4.1 genre pattern "growth bought per-step, steep scaling" (Strong); *Judgment:* exact drain curves (ECOQ-002).
*Success criterion:* paper check — the §17 worked examples' per-turn currency deltas reconcile exactly (§17's conservation checks); §18's activity matrix shows no wealth-accelerating loop without a standing drain; ECOQ-002 remains open for numeric budgeting.

**EG-8. Recoverability after mistakes, poor conditions, or inactivity.**
*Stance:* failure = solvency floor events (SDQ-007's austerity family), *not* confiscation of identity/standing; returns-unrestored production resumes automatically; a respecialization path exists (EG-5).
*Maps:* DP-001/DP-002, F-012, SDQ-007, Persona Mira.
*Evidence:* Prompt 2's fatality-avoidance family (F-012; eRep's churn evidence via Prompt 1 §3.5's cautionary note is Moderate); *Judgment:* floor mechanics (SDQ-007 open — the posture here is directional: austerity reduces volatility, never wipe-restarts).
*Success criterion:* §17C/D worked examples show a *modelled damaged* nation reaching pre-crisis production within a modeled bounded arc; ECV-005's failure-case sweep (§18.2) records no state leaving a sim nation permanently unable to act.

**EG-9. Understandable commitments, upkeep, and projected balances.**
*Stance:* the finance view's commitment-reserve model (§6.1; §15) states upcoming drains as line-items; multi-turn arc orders (MO-008) show per-turn milesteffect and end-of-arc balance; projections always labeled forecast with change-hazards listed.
*Maps:* DP-004, AP-006, F-019 (unpredictable obligations as drain-churn), SYS-017; Persona Mira.
*Evidence:* F-010's confusion family; *Judgment:* the preview-precision envelope (Prompt 6 §9B already fixed the existence of consequence-previews at concept level;PXO-002).
*Success criterion:* ECV-001's forecast-legibility criterion (§15.6): players can state the *next three turns'* projected netchange for their own nation from only visible data; accuracy target is qualitative at paper stage ("no hidden surprise line items in §17's examples") — a quantitative miss-tolerance would be invented without playtest evidence, so it is not.

**EG-10. Defenses against collusion, alternate accounts, automation, and exploitation.**
*Stance:* preventive invariants (atomic transfers, determinism, rate limits, reservation rules) outrank detective tooling; the R-register (§13) names every risk with a prevention-first control and a deferral phase.
*Maps:* FO-009's §8 warning, R-01/R-02/R-07/R-12 lineage + new R-13…R-21, CCR-008, Persona Kwame (future tier) as adversarially-designed-against.
*Evidence:* P&W's automation/young-nation trade rules verified (Prompt 1/3 registers [FV]) — the genre's proven regulatory shape; *Judgment:* our rule set (ECOQ-006).
*Success criterion:* every §13 risk row carries prevention + detection + response + false-positive assessment + phase-assignment; the §17E manipulation example demonstrates both what is prevented and what is merely detected.

**EG-11. Trade/diplomacy useful, not mandatory.**
*Stance:* independent nations reach every Tier-A system (AP-004); trade creates records that *help* relationships (INT-004) but no mechanic *requires* trading to reach any success channel's *minimum*; all alliance participation stays optional.
*Maps:* DP-005 (bounded by DP-001), AP-004, F-013's forced-alliance critique, Persona Mira, SDQ-015.
*Evidence:* F-013's forced-alliance complaint (Moderate); *Judgment:* the exact minimum functionality standard.
*Success criterion:* the paper calculus (§17B) shows autarky is *achievable but materially inferior* (not "illegal, not impossible, not "confiscated-but-difficult"); V-8's beta test keeps the socialization question honest.

**EG-12. Low operational and content-maintenance burden.**
*Stance:* 4 material classes + a tiny record taxonomy + batched per-turn writes + no per-nation journal flood (§16); the market stage's cost gate is enforced by a written condition list (§10.4), not vibes.
*Maps:* AP-003, SIM-003/SIM-006, D-005/SDQ-003, CCR-008/CCR-010.
*Evidence:* D1 free-tier figures (verified §16.4); Prompt 3 §8's per-opportunity cost table [FV/analyst]; *Judgment:* the batched-write strategy.
*Success criterion:* the §16.3 framework's per-turn write formula, when its "realistic small-world" assumption set is applied, stays inside 100k row-writes/day at the modeled turn frequency — with the model's assumptions explicit and the falsifying write-per-turn number stated (§16.3).

### 5.2 How the economy serves the provisional CX-5 identity

CX-5 ("The Community of Nations," provisional, not approved) asserts that nations benefit from their own distinctive strengths while relationships remain meaningful. The economy is the load-bearing half of that claim:

- **"Distinctive strengths"** — development-line emphasis (MO-001) is the *mechanical* home of a national identity: a nation's mixture of payments (upkeep), investments, and sourcing choices differs from every other's without any hard-coded classes or geographic endowments. §7's decision table defines what makes each line's role non-interchangeable.
- **"Relationships remain meaningful"** — efficiency asymmetry (§9) makes surplus-selling an ongoing structural advantage rather than a vending-machine side activity; each completed directed trade writes a relationship record (INT-004; SDQ-015's pending question is *how* it reads), so the trade network *is* a social graph with a memory.
- **"Not a production spreadsheet"** — the identity's anti-spreadsheet reading (rejected CX-1; Prompt 5 RC-2's preserved logic) is honored by keeping the decision surface at ~1 allocation + ~1 respecialization + trade reactions per session, not by simulating consumer demand (FO-021 Deferred) or layers of per-building math (SIM-002's rejection of cities/buildings/population).
- **Where CX-5 is put under pressure:** the economy alone cannot *deliver* CX-5's community feel — it needs §9.5's social-value mechanisms to translate structural gaps into conversations, treaties, and trust records; and it must be re-validated against CV-003 (multi-path perception) and CV-002 (mapless world reading) rather than assumed. Recorded in §23A's pillar audit.

### 6.4 The financial lifecycle (Deliverable 2, conceptual flow)

**Starting resources → recurring income & production → available treasury & materials → investments & upkeep → trades & transfers → turn resolution → records & explanations.**

1. **Start:** a new nation receives starting funds + starter line levels; SYS-018's onboarding teaches the *first allocation* (Prompt 5 §15's E1 posture).
2. **Each boundary:** SYS-003 computes output (materials to stocks, income to treasury); drains deduct; committed orders resolve in SDQ-018's deterministic catalog order; failures release reservations with reasons.
3. **Between boundaries:** the player queues investments, arc orders, emphasis changes, trade offers; each reserves/satisfies affordability checks at queue time (§14).
4. **Trades:** directed transfers execute on acceptance (immediate-with-record, both-sided atomic — §14.2), writing relationship records (INT-004).
5. **After resolution:** the national digest reports every delta with provenance (SYS-017); the finance view updates available/committed; the chronicle receives notable entries (SDQ-017's quality bar).
6. **Long-run:** line levels accumulate; drains grow; emphasis drifts or is deliberately revised; reserves exist as *stockpiles with uses*, not dead weight (§8's MAT roles) — and the inflation distribution question gets periodic review (ECOQ-002's budget-loop check).

### 7.3 Why not six lines (Science as a line) — and the omission is deliberate

The Prompt 6 candidate list named **Science** among six candidates. ### 7.4 Thresholds and progression — what a level-up *means*

The task's five distinctions, and how the design expresses each (all positional, none "just numbers up"):

- **More raw output** — the *least* rewarded form: levels raise output roughly linearly while **drains scale at least as fast** (§12's anti-snowball core), so raw level ≠ runaway advantage.
- **Greater efficiency** — works/program-class modifiers reduce *drain-per-level*, expressing "mature administration"; capped (no compound loops).
- **New capabilities** — the *primary* threshold currency: option-class unlocks (market posture, arc slots, focus classes, market gate participation). Prompt 6 §19's posture preserved: "progress is expressed in options, not only totals."
- **Better resilience** — WRK's defensive outputs; never expresses as *offensive* power (DP-006 line). Reserved-cap vs shock-count in §9.4's chokepoint tests.
- **Different choices** — emphasis reallocation itself is the "different choices" mechanism (§11), priced by SDQ-016 rather than free.

**Anti-ladder check (the disguised-ladder failure the task names):** a threshold that *selects who may compete* (e.g., "only level-10+ nations may trade rare outputs") is **rejected** — thresholds unlock *tools*, never *participation floors*, with one documented exception noted as such: the **approval-based listing-market gate** (§10.4) gates a *feature*, not a success path, and its criteria are behavioral/integrity-based, not level-based. Newcomer thresholds stay *low and early* so that "unlocking the interesting choices" does happen within the first sessions (V-2's P0 concern).

### 7.5 Interchangeability discipline (anti "same numbers, different labels")

Reasons the five lines are not dressed-up identical columns:
1. **They consume different material inputs and produce different classes** (§8's mapping) — a COM-heavy nation cannot serve as a WRK-heavy nation's supplier for MAT-02.
2. **Only two lines create tradeable surplus** (PRO, WRK); one creates *capacity* (ADM); one creates *margin* (COM); one creates *defense floor* (RDY) — four distinct economic roles, all necessary in *some* quantity, none necessary in *every* strategy (GR-2's disciplines).
3. **Their threshold opinions are disjoint by design** — §7.2's row; §18's dominance scan hunts down accidental collisions.

## 8. Tradeable material classes (Deliverable 4 — SDQ-002)

### 8.1 Examining the "6–10 classes" starting hypothesis — and reducing it

The Prompt 6 hypothesis suggested ~6–10. On examination, that range was a *ceiling* to avoid over-trimming, not a target. Every additional class costs: player comprehension (AP-006), interface burden, D1 storage (§16), balance surface (each class needs ≥2 uses, ≥2 sourcing routes, ≥2 buyer classes — the Prompt 6 §9C structural test), and *thin-market risk* at small world sizes (with 8 classes × a 15-nation world, most class-pair markets would be empty). The **minimum useful set** is small: production diversity must be *able to* create specialized supply/demand asymmetries; with 4 well-shaped classes, a 10-nation world already sustains several complementary pairing shapes (§18.3 tests lower bounds). **Four launch classes are recommended** (ECON-006), one deferred expansion class documented (§8.5). ### 8.4 The structural test applied (Prompt 6's rule, made concrete)

Every meaningful capability gap solvable by a material has ≥2 viable sourcing routes (self-production at lower efficiency counts as one), and every important surplus has ≥2 plausible buyer classes. §9.4 runs the full two-route test per class. **Recorded now:** the test passes *structurally* for all four classes at the paper level (§9.4's table); whether the *tuned* efficiency asymmetries are large enough to make trading real is the numeric open question ECOQ-004.

### 8.5 The deferred fifth class (and why four launch)

A fifth class family — *consumer/prestige goods* (a "quality-of-life/luxury" class that could tie into domestic politics (SYS-006) and diplomacy-status displays) — is **deferred**, not rejected: FO-021 (consumer demand) is Deferred; SYS-006 is a minimal v1 system; a fifth class would add comprehension load for marginal models-first value. **Revisit condition:** if SYS-006's stability levers or the diplomatic status registers *need* an economic input class to be meaningful, the fifth class gets designed explicitly at that stage. (ECOQ-011 records it.)

### 8.6 Deliberate anti-patterns recorded

- No class exists to "be accumulated and sold" without a use (the task's resource-identity rule): all four consume *every turn* (MAT-01) or price *every build* (MAT-02) or *both* are invested/converted strands (MAT-03/04).
- No class is "trade-only" (a from-real-countries intuition explicitly rejected by the task): e.g., MAT-04's deterrence use is *domestic-pricing* value.
- Not scattering one real resource into many labels: the four classes were chosen over a "raw/manufactured two-tier + variants" because the *manufactured* split's real jobs (floor, capital, versatile, consumable) are the actual economic axes here.

## 9. Structural scarcity and economic interdependence (Deliverable 5 — SDQ-009)

This is Prompt 7's central design requirement, per the task.

### 9.1 The answer to "acquire distinctive strengths without a map" — the asymmetry engine

Because there is no geography to differentiate nations, *every differentiation must be purchased by prior choices*. The engine:

1. **Every nation can make every material — slowly.** An unspecialized nation produces any class at a generic low efficiency; a *levered + emphasized + focused* line produces the same class much more efficiently (ECON-011's terms; the exact efficiency curves are ECOQ-004's open numbers — *directional certainty, numeric uncertainty*, recorded honestly).
2. **Importing is cheaper than building — at the margin.** If the efficiency gap is real, buying a specialist's surplus is economically rational even for a nation that *could* eventually self-produce. This makes trade an *economy*, not a courtesy — and makes it *structural* (DP-005's letter) without any artificial "must trade" rule (DP-001's bound).
3. **Emphasis is costly to change** (§11), so the asymmetry is *sticky* — sticky enough for a trade network to form, gentle enough to escape (the §11 balance pair).
4. **Thresholds compound the shape** — they put *option* value on committed emphasis; they never erase the ability to pivot (no build traps; §7.4).
5. **Complement agreements (MO-002's pairing niches) create the cooperative layer:** two nations whose emphasis patterns are complementary can sign a *dividend agreement* — a small, capped, mutual efficiency-price bonus on *identified material pairs* (lineages: CN's trade circles [FV-lite, Prompt 1/3 registers]; re-derived fully mapless). Cap: modest (the bonus must never render underspecialized production competitive with it — otherwise specialization collapses into "everyone signs 4 pairings"). SDQ-015-family work (relationship economics) and the numbers stage tune it.

**Why not rely purely on trade-only bonuses/mandatory trade (the task's explicit cautions):** both edges collapse specialization into ledger arithmetic. The asymmetry engine puts the *structural* load on production inefficiency (the honest root cause: nobody is great at everything), with bonuses/politics as the *social* layer — not the structural one. (§20.1's comparison table lists the alternatives' failure cases.)

### 9.2 Autarky analysis (task 9.A)

The question: *can a nation efficiently develop every capability?* The design answer: **efficient autarky is not available; autarky is merely available** — self-production of all four classes at generic efficiency plus unspecialized thresholds is *solvent* (DP-001-bounded AP-004's independence) but *materially inferior* to trading with complementary partners (the §17B worked example quantifies the illustrative gap). Key guards against both failure edges:

- **If self-sufficiency became *cheap*** (efficiency gap too small): trade dies into decoration (CCR-005's opposite edge). Guard: the numeric stage must verify the gap is large; ECV-001's two-nation paper test already bites on it structurally.
- **If autarky became *impossible*** (hard material exclusions): a nation caught by one embargoed supplier gets helpless (CCR-005's named risk). Guard: no class is ever *producible nowhere else* (the two-route test's hard floor); generic low-efficiency production is a *right*, not an unlock (thresholds unlock *options*, not basic self-production — §7.4's anti-participation-floor rule).

### 9.5 Social and political value (task 9.D) — how scarcity becomes society

The mechanisms by which *structural gaps* generate *international life* (without turning every player into a trader):

1. **Bilateral trade creates relationship capital:** every completed directed trade writes a paired history record (INT-004); SDQ-015's open shape question is whether the register reads numerically or descriptively — either way, reliability *compounds into better predictable partners*: the trade web becomes a trust web (Persona Ade's territory; trade *starts* as economics and becomes diplomacy).
2. **Dividend/complement agreements (MO-002) are *legible mutualisms*:** visible, capped pairing bonuses make each nation's economic role *declare itself* to the world — a mapless nation's "where we sit" appears (DP-003's duty: the dependency map *is* the world view's economic layer).
3. **Alliances gain economic objects without mandatory membership:** shared reserve pools, collective deterrence budgets (Tier C) — voluntary (AP-004); benefits exist but no success channel *requires* them.
4. **Non-traders still live in the network:** the digest tells a never-trading nation what the market around them shifts on (price/posture records — visible at the world level per SDQ-004's visibility policy); scarcity pressure is *felt* even when not *traded on*, which is honest tension without chore (DP-002 check; F-019 border).
5. **Conflict economics stay visible and bounded** (DP-006's line): indifferences this far — the war stage owns them but *this* economy supplies its inputs (MAT-04, the drain structure) and holds the joint guarantee (no economy collapse via boundary war spikes — §13.4's exchange with the war stage).

## 11. Emphasis and respecialization (Deliverable 7 — SDQ-016, MO-001)

### 11.1 What emphasis actually changes (the required explanation #1)

Emphasis (MO-001's mechanism, adopted): each development line has an **emphasis weight** — a *relative* allocation of the nation's growth momentum. Effects (all qualitative shapes, all numbers open):

- **Per-line output efficiency** — concentrated paperwork effort yields better per-level yield (asymmetry engine's root, §9.1-1).
- **Speed of progress** — a heavily-weighted line gains levels faster *from the same investment budget*.

All lines technically remain available at all times (DP-001's letter; no line "closes" from neglect — it just progresses slower).

6. **How long old config persists:** until the boundary applies the new one — the *actual* old config (never blended/concurrent).
7. **Does the change weaken output temporarily:** yes — the **retooling dip**: brief efficiency lull while output re-orients (bounded, digest-explained: one value, one reason, computed from the new-vs-old weights gap). The dip is the *fairness* counterweight to wealthy nations otherwise swapping risk-free (EG-5's escape mechanism for newcomers must not read as "free" to veterans; it isn't: cost + distance + dip are the price of identity change).
8. **Anti price-flip arbitrage:** distance-gate + boundary-queue mean a change can never chase a *this-turn* price; plus fee+distance costs make weekly flavor-flipping uneconomic; R-17's regime-watcher provides detection for the residual — see R-17's row (§13).
9. **Newcomer not trapped:** distance-gates are *short* at low total line investment (the gate scales with total invested mass, not turn count per-player) — an early mistake costs one arc of retooling, not permanent identity loss; a dip's value is bounded regardless of nation age, so a **young** nation's retrain is cheap and **old** nations' retrain is expensive-but-possible (DP-001's letter preserved at every size; no permanent trap).
10. **Offline/inactive nations:** emphasis carries no decay and no expiry — an inactive nation's economy resolves its upkeep with unchanged identity; retooling while offline is fine (queued + scheduled = absence-tolerant), and SDQ-016's answer is cadence-clean (no mechanic assumes attendance, DP-002).

## 12. Inflation, accumulation, and economic snowballing (Deliverable 8)

### 12.1 The currency accounting framework (task-required) — four categories, one identity

| Category | Definition | Effect on total circulating currency | Economy controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 Created by system rules** | Production income; starting grants; designated event-scale adjustments; (market-stage none — fees destroy, not transfer) | **+** | The *sole* expansion channel; grows only when nations invest — investment must first *pay* (damp: creation pulls prior spend) |
| **C2 Transferred between players** | Directed trades; alliance pool flows; treaty payments; settlement indemnities (war stage) | **Zero net effect. Transfers redistribute, never inflate.** | Symmetric atomicity (R-19) — no transfer loses or gains a unit; every transfer creates two ledger records (audit trail) |
| **C3 Removed by system costs** | Upkeep drains; investment spends; (fees, if the market ever introduces them) | **–** | Drains scale with levels (mature nations destroy proportionally more); investment spend *creates value elsewhere* (levels) but removes liquidity |
| **C4 Held as reserves/commitments** | Earmarked reserves; queued-commitment reservations | Zero supply effect; *velocity* effect only (held-out-of-circulation) | Reservation release/failure refund is C2's mirror (must also net zero) |

**The identity each boundary satisfies (§17's worked checks instantiate it):** `Total circ_{T+1} = Total circ_T + C1 – C3`. And per-nation: `Treasury_{T+1} = Treasury_T + (own C1 share) – (own C3) – (sent C2) + (received C2)`. Commitments are C4, resolved via §14's catalog before this arithmetic runs (so the numbers in the digest exclude *reserved-but-unreleased* — the financial preview matches §15.4's boundary view). *No transaction in the tested mistakes increases supply while claiming to be a transfer* — the framework's whole point (the task's explicit don't-confuse-transfers-with-creation warning).

**Materials have an analogous ledger** (four classes, same shape): produced into stocks (creation), consumed by drains/investment (destruction), trade transfers (redistribution), stock reserves (held). §17's examples account both sides.

### 12.3 The snowball look — honestly

Wealth's actual *snowball* in this design: more treasury → more investment capacity → higher levels → more income *and* proportionally higher drains → *linear-ish* net advantage, *plus* option-unlocks → richer plays but not *richer-per-crown* returns (capped efficiency modifiers). The remaining genuine accelerations to watch: (i) threshold-chain effects (an early unlock enabling a *second* early unlock) — audited in §18.1; (ii) market-stage information edges (reference-price literacy) — G-4's review; (iii) alliance staging — R-08/§13's bounded pooling. Both failure modes stay *explicitly on the register*: neither "wealth guarantees everything" nor "wealth means nothing" is acceptable (the task's don't-solve-by-irrelevance line).

### 12.4 Where the economy could become dominant despite multiple paths

The honest exposure list: (a) currency thresholds located on *every* other success channel's input path — audited per-channel in §23; (b) income compelling perpetual growth — drains' scaling is the countermeasure and its weakest assumption is that drains *feel* fair (a numbers-stage death trap if implemented lazily — ECOQ-002's tuning risk named); (c) trade specialists absorbing all surplus — the pairing-cap + relationship-credit terms keep margins relationship-priced (SDQ-015's open shape question is a *tracking* register item; if resolved to "no memory," margins become purely scale-priced and the dominance risk rises — noted in ECOQ-007's review).

### 12.5 Newcomer and recovery boundaries (the FO-018 discipline — NOT resolved)

The task forbids silently closing FO-018. Recorded boundary conditions that *any* future catch-up policy must respect (ECON-012 carries it, §22.2):

1. It must not create a *reward for being new* (inverts v1's fairness stance and invites protection-fishing, F-017's family).
2. It must not be implemented as *pure stat-gift* (breaks DP-001: tokens of "earned" standing become purchasable identity).
3. It should leverage, not counterfeit, the existing asymmetry engine — e.g., emphasis costs that scale with invested mass (already newcomer-cheap, §11.3-9) are *the honest lever in place*; a purely separate veteran/newcomer tax is an alternative FO-018 must compare.
4. It must not violate DP-002 (no attendance mechanics), AP-004 (no forced alliances), and must treat *forms of succession* (alliance-scale newcomers) identically to solo ones unless defined per-apology scope.
5. **Its existence implies the economy track a *newcomer-participation metric*** (whether new nations' trade/activity rates approximate the population mean long-term) — measurable, no invented threshold; ECOQ-007's playtest-era review owns the read.

Where the economy design *depends on* FO-018 unresolved: starting-grant size (ECOQ-003), optional legacy-cost/depreciation posture (ECOQ-008), and the catch-up scoped *variance between* v worlds of different ages (only observable in playtest). Recorded not-forgotten: §22.4's gate row.

### 13.2 New economy-integrity risks (R-13…R-21)

**R-13 — Multi-account circular trading (activity/wealth laundering via self-plays).**
- *Objective:* inflate a growing nation with a main account's wealth or a fabricated activity record, without "paying" for it.
- *Conditions:* two+ accounts; trade layer; no transfer caps; records that "count" activity.
- *Sequence:* A→B→A circular transfers; or deliberately unbalanced A→B gift chains across pairs; repeat.
- *Benefit:* velocity of activity appearance; wealth placement inside one player's control.
- *Damage:* distributed-flexibility loss (inflated activity stats pervert G-3's measurements; wealth arrives without gameplay).
- *Prevention:* one-way transfer caps (per-turn/per-pair value caps at *submission*, not detection); circular-pair **rate limits per pairing** (a pair's trade velocity band); the reserve-and-execute model matches atomicity so "spearing" attacks can't exceed caps mid-flight; identity/maturity restrictions (R-01 extension).
- *Detection:* per-pair counter flow balance anomalies; escalating across-pair circular-graph signals (a counting-only check — no behavioral model).
- *Response:* cap breaches flagged for audit review (not auto-punishment); review's response classes: tighten caps, transaction block on the *pair*, incident record.
- *False-positive/privacy risk:* legitimate inter-alliance mutual aid could breach velocity caps — caps set above *realistic* allied-trade volume, published; privacy: per-pair analytics reviewed only in audit-trail context, no profile scoring.
- *Complexity/cost:* moderate (counter data already exists in trade records).
- *Phase:* required before public economy (with R-01's suite).

**R-14 — Coordinated price manipulation (market-stage).**
- *Objective:* move the day-end reference price to profit from a pre-placed position or to slow a rival's buy.
- *Conditions:* requires the staged listing market; multiple colluding accounts or one big player.
- *Sequence:* pre-place own-position → coordinated synchronous-filled fake-liquidity trades/aggressive listings at manipulated prices → day-end drift's averages shift → profit or disinfo the reference layer.
- *Benefit:* broken reference-price trust; margin farming.
- *Damage:* new players mis-set their expectations; real producers mis-plan; the trade view's promises (DP-004) break.
- *Prevention:* reference layer computed from *completed-transaction-weighted* quotes excluding self-pair circular volume (R-13's graph signal); aggregates from *day windows, not spot*; wash-signal filter run before publication; declared max-buys-mins for repeat offenders.
- *Detection:* distribution-anomaly signals on day-end inputs (giant single-pair volumes; bid/ask spread spike patterns); all *counts*-based, no identity inference.
- *Response:* G-4 review owns: expose to public a "reference-influence events" note when anomalous-day signals trigger; in worst cases, a day's reference is published with a *caveat flag* (honesty beat fake-stability).
- *False-positive risk:* legitimate big real-trades could trip the flag — criteria favor consensus-style filters; a flagged day degrades gracefully ("low-confidence reference" label), never punishes traders.
- *Complexity/cost:* high — *this cost is one of the reasons the market gate exists* (§10.4's G-4).
- *Phase:* required before market introduction.

**R-15 — Market cornering / bottleneck cartelization.**
- *Objective:* lock up a class's supply and charge availability premiums, harming non-cartel nations' solvency.
- *Conditions:* one class's efficient producers concentrated; small worlds (few sources).
- *Sequence:* accumulate MAT-M stocks; embargo/directed trades to friendly pairs only; or collusive listings.
- *Benefit:* availability premiums; political leverage.
- *Damage:* the targeted nations' arcs stall; newcomers' model of "what is real" breaks; honest scarcity *degenerates into* siege.
- *Prevention:* §9's *structural* answers: the universal generic-efficiency self-route (no class is *exclusively* producible anywhere); the two-route test as standing test (design review); reserve-stock rules; cross-route switching class (§9.3's commitment);
- *Detection:* supply-concentration signals (world stockshare by nation/pair, vs world consumption mass) — a *published* world-statistic (DP-004: concentration itself is visible info, not a secret — the *judge* here is the world's own eyes).
- *Response:* honest-market état: corners are *legal* under route-diversity; the design's promised protection is *routes + buffers + switching*; R-15's row records it as a *control* to monitor not a *crime* to hunt.
- *False-positive risk:* n/a (statistics are public).
- *Complexity/cost:* low (public stat).
- *Phase:* design-time structural test + playtest watch.

**R-16 — One-sided/unfair trades (wealth transfer disguised as trade).**
- *Objective:* funnel treasury from a compromised/novice/sabotaged party to another.
- *Conditions:* unwary/outnumbered counterpart; term magnitude unexamined; multi-account crossovers.
- *Sequence:* massively unbalanced offer dressed as reasonable-looking (or exploiting a novice / a prize-offer to a compromised account).
- *Benefit:* unearned wealth placement.
- *Damage:* the target loses treasury; community trust in trade terms decays.
- *Prevention + structurally-relevant controls:* term-magnitude *asymmetry cap per pair per period* (caps the *imbalance* a pair can push through, regardless of "willingness"); imbalance disclosure preview ("this trade transfers 85% of X's treasury — confirm?") for the *weaker* side; new-account incoming-trade limits (R-01's suite); audit trail on anything above the imbalance band.
- *Detection:* post-hoc audit review of flagged-pair anomaly records.
- *Response:* conservative (audit → rule adjustment), never confiscation-first (no trade-confiscation mechanics); the *responses* live in the rules' tightening, not in policing adults' agreements.
- *False-positive risk:* legitimate unequal trades (gifts among friends are a *corroborated real behavior class* — CN/P&W communities encourage them) — thresholds set high enough that gifts under a declared band pass without friction; the band is a *published rule*, not a judgment call.
- *Privacy note:* imbalance-band analytics reviewed per-pair in audit context only.
- *Complexity/cost:* moderate (existing records suffice).
- *Phase:* required before public economy (a weaker-sender protection exists at launch — the newcomer-trap vector is real from day one).

**R-17 — Emphasis-switch arbitrage / regime-flipping games.**
- *Objective:* exploit fast reweighting to chase short-term price/threshold windows (e.g., flip into MAT-04 buyers just before a crisis, flip back right after).
- *Conditions:* respecialization too cheap/fast; public price signals exist.
- *Sequence:* monitor; bid flips at window edges; never bear the asymmetry-earned cost others paid.
- *Benefit:* margin without stickiness promised; specialization's *whole premise* (§9) dies when weights are liquid.
- *Damage:* scarcity engine collapses; honest specialists become marks.
- *Prevention (the actual design):* §11's three-part cost (fee + distance-gate + boundary-queue + dip) is the specific R-17 countermeasure — *prevention by regulation, not detection*; no behavioral scoring required.
- *Detection:* regime-flip velocity signals (only for tuning review, not for individual surveillance).
- *Response:* numeric stage tunes the gates; no individualized response (the rule is the protection).
- *False-positive risk:* n/a (rule-level).
- *Phase:* required at concept-test (the §11 rules ship with the economy).

**R-18 — Order replay / cancellation-spam / queue flooding.**
- *Objective:* enumerate/cancel molecules at a boundary to fish for races, grief a rival's preview data, or inflate processing load.
- *Conditions:* high edit rate on queued orders near boundaries; no rate guard.
- *Sequence:* submit/cancel cycles in the boundary window; or repeated identical submissions (dup detection stress).
- *Benefit:* race-fishing; rival digest noise; infrastructure drag.
- *Prevention:* submit/cancel rate caps (per nation per window, modest and published); queue-editing freezes in the final minutes of a boundary (a short, published "commitment freeze" window — keeps races honest and lets the tick run deterministically); idempotency keys on order submission (duplicate submissions of the same logical order collapse to one — SDQ-018's tech clause).
- *Detection:* rate-cap breach logs (rule-generated, not behavioral).
- *Response:* hard cap application (the rate limit simply applies); incident records for ops review; no account scoring.
- *False-positive risk:* rapid-genuine replanning (a player restaging their whole queue) — cap set generously above legitimate edit cadence; freezes are short and visible.
- *Privacy:* n/a.
- *Complexity/cost:* low-moderate (rate limiter + freeze window).
- *Phase:* required at concept-test (the engine must be stable for the concept test's R-07's suite to hold).

**R-19 — Partial-failure value leakage.**
- *Objective:* (not an attacker's Union first — an engine-fault class the attacker *can* provoke): provoke a mid-flight failure so resources move without their counterpart of the barter, or vice-versa; or the accept executes but the transfer partially lands.
- *Conditions:* non-atomic implementation; reservation not enforced.
- *Sequence:* accept while insufficient; boundary resolves one leg not the other; races between submit/queue/accept orders.
- *Benefit:* free value (unpaid leg).
- *Damage:* honest parties lose; distrust (DP-004 breaks).
- *Prevention:* the **zero-partial invariant** as an engine assert: either both legs commit, or neither does, in *one* atomic step; reservation model (§14.2) prevents the insufficient-at-execution class; SDQ-018's catalog is the race decoder.
- *Detection:* invariant violation is a *build-breaking* assert (engine-level, not statistical).
- *Response:* none needed at runtime (invariant holds); dev-stage tests assert it (§17F's worked example documents expectation).
- *False-positive risk:* n/a.
- *Complexity/cost:* build discipline (not a separate system).
- *Phase:* required at concept-test.

**R-20 — Alliance-level economic concentration (shared-pool power distortions).**
- *Objective:* centralize sufficient wealth/materials in an alliance pool to dominate world supply or run proxy manipulation (Tier C's SYS-010 exists for honest pooling).
- *Conditions:* Tier-C alliance tooling live; pooling permits; weak audit trail.
- *Sequence:* mass contributions (voluntary) → pool-directed manipulation (R-14-class actions sourced from the pool) or pool-driven embargoes.
- *Benefit:* pooled market power.
- *Damage:* smaller nations/independents' solvency; AP-004's non-mandatory stance is coerced economically (membership pressure).  
- *Prevention:* pool transfer/withdrawal fully audit-logged (R-08's audit-first culture is *the* control — the FO-007 differentiator); pool-level volume caps (modest) + source-tag of pool-originated trade records (pool trades *lobby as pool* — visible as pool, not as member anonymity); contribution volume is crisis-auditable.
- *Detection:* pool-concentration world-statistics (public, per R-15's visibility).
- *Response:* caps + audit + (worst-case) pool-command limit — all rule-policy, no member-policing inside alliances (internal governance is *their* culture; the game provides *transparency*, not governance).
- *False-positive risk:* legitimate deterrence pools (genuinely useful collective behavior) — caps sized generously; the *world-visible* statistic is the intended check, not a suspicion engine.
- *Phase:* with alliance boot (Tier C).

**R-21 — Exploiting absent/abandoned counterparties.**
- *Objective:* loot or manipulate a nation whose player is inactive (offer spam, squeeze pricing to an absent specialist's surplus, test artificial suspension of their power for timing exploitation).  
- *Conditions:* FO-015's absence handling live; lack of "inactive" protections; absence reports not digested.
- *Sequence:* identify absence signs (or wait for digest's "last seen" indicators) → exploit the market position/supply glut of that nation.
- *Benefit:* free leverage; squeeze pricing without resistance.
- *Damage:* the returning player's economy bears squeezed-term damage; the community's absence-fairness trust (the whole design of Prompt 4, Section 9, item 8 — “Missing several turns”) breaks.
- *Prevention:* absence-design integration (FO-015-class mechanisms: pending-actions continue resolving per the standing rules; market-stage restrictions on trading *with* long-suspended nations — P28-class young-account restrictions' cousin, applied to *suspended* states; squeeze pricing against an absent party is visible via the trade view's reference info);
- *Detection:* asymmetric pattern signals (profit concentrated against suspended partners) — audit-context only;
- *Response:* the absence-system's own rules (not this economy's bets); incident records for ops review;
- *False-positive risk:* misreading a *slow* player as absent; the absence protections are the FO-015 design's job — this row is an integration requirement *on* that design, not its replacement.
- *Phase:* required before public economy (absence design ships early with the concept test; FO-015's own status is Candidate).

## 14. Two-hour turns and economic resolution (Deliverable 10)

### 14.1 Classification under the three-class contract (SYS-016 / PXO-003)

Every economic action classified per the Prompt 6 contract (immediate-with-record / queued-until-boundary / boundary-scheduled).

| Economic action | Class | Effective when | Reversible before resolution? | Affordability checked | Reserved? | Record | Failure policy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change emphasis | Immediate-with-record submit; effect queued to the next boundary | Order recorded on submit; weights apply at the next boundary | Yes (cancel before the boundary) | Respec fee checked at submit AND at the boundary | Fee reserved on submit | Emphasis-change order + application record (INT-010) | Fee unaffordable at boundary → change fails, reservation released, old emphasis remains |
| Invest in a line | Queued-until-boundary | At boundary | Yes, cancel until boundary | At submit (cost reserved) AND at boundary | Cost reserved at submit | Investment order + result record | Reserved but unpayable → order fails, reservation released (no partial spend) |
| Initiate trade offer | Immediate-with-record | On submit | Yes, cancel before acceptance | Offer is a pledge, not a transfer | No reservation until acceptance (one side of an unresolved conflict — see DEF-018 at §10.2) | Offer record (per-pair state) | Never fails from counterparty state; own-side solvency floor applies (R-13) |
| Accept trade | Immediate-with-record (multi-write atomic) | On accept | No — atomic pair | At accept (both sides) | Both sides reserved then released atomically | Per-pair record both sides (INT-011) | Atomic pair — zero partial transfer (Section 10.6); duplicate accept idempotent |
| Decline/expire/counteroffer | Immediate-with-record | On action / expiry | n/a | n/a | No reservation | Offer-state transition | n/a |
| Pay upkeep | Boundary-scheduled | At boundary | No (auto) | At boundary | n/a | Digest summary line | Deficit → policy ladder (§6.4), deterministic |
| Emergency levy | Immediate, one per emergency | On submit | No (policy ladder binds) | Floor checked now | n/a | Ledger entry (large, flagged) | Above floor → rejected with reason |
| Receive production/income | Boundary-scheduled | At boundary | n/a | n/a | n/a | Digest summary lines | n/a (automatic) |
| Convert to/from strategic reserve | Immediate-with-record | On submit | Yes | Balance checked | Balance moves between C1 and reserve | Ledger entry | Unaffordable → rejected |

The classes are the Prompt 6 contract applied verbatim — no new timing class is created for the economy.

### 14.2 Boundary correctness principles

1. **Deterministic ordered resolution.** All boundary-scheduled economic processing uses one explicit ordering rule — SDQ-018 (boundary ordering and idempotency) owns *the general ordering question*; this report only fixes the economy's place in it: economic processing is a single phase of the boundary, inputs read from pre-phase state, outputs written once. Conflicting orders between two economic actions of the same nation in one boundary are resolved by explicit submission order recorded at submit time (timestamped, monotonic) — not by re-deriving at execution.
2. **The preview tells the truth.** A financial preview must state the conditions under which an ordered action *will* resolve ("will execute at boundary 12:00 providing the nation still has ≥ X on hand at that boundary; failure policy: X"). If a preview cannot know future conditions (market-dependent affordability, counterparty action), it must say so explicitly ("conditional — may fail at boundary if …"). A preview that presents a conditional outcome as guaranteed is a DP-004 violation.
3. **Idempotency for retries.** (Abstract-level, per SDQ-018's sell to a later technical stage): every boundary economic write is safe to retry — a retry of an already-completed resolution is a no-op, not a double execution. What the technical stage needs from this report: economic state is describable as (balance snapshot, per-order applied/not-applied flag); application must be a single atomic unit.
4. **Failure pre-states vs mid-resolution surprises.** The economy's rule: affordability is evaluated *twice* — once when a player submits (reservation), once when the boundary action actually executes (re-check against actual balance at boundary). Between those moments the world may change (income shocks, trades executed by others, upkeeps); the ordered action's *failure policy* is fixed at submit and shown to the player, not redecided at execution time.

### 14.3 What belongs to the economy stage vs the technical stage

| Problem | Owner |
|---|---|
| What counts as "affordable" (spendable vs reserved) | Economy stage — defined here (§6) |
| Fixed failure policy per ordered action, shown to player | Economy stage — defined here (§14.1) |
| Double-spend across two concurrent trades | Economy stage — rule defined (atomic pair, reservations, solvency floor; Section 10.6); enforcement implementation | Technical stage (SDQ-018 work) |
| Ordering of economic actions vs military, politics, research at one boundary | Turn engine (SDQ-018) — economy supplies only its single-phase requirement and submission-order tiebreak |
| Retries, partial-write protection, transaction boundaries, D1 write batching | Technical stage |
| What the digest line says when a trade fails | Economy stage (message contract demonstrated in §17F) |
| Duplicate order submission (user clicks twice) | Economy rule: second submit of an identical unacted offer is rejected as duplicate (same pair, same terms, not yet responded) with an explanation — no hidden merge. Technical stage provides the uniqueness check. |

This split preserves D-005's neutrality: nothing here assumes a specific provider's transaction semantics.

### 14.4 Turn cadence fit (Deliverable 10 fit check)

| Cadence expectation | Economic answer |
|---|---|
| Nothing requires being online at a boundary | Correct: income, production, upkeep, ordered investments resolve automatically; trades execute atomically on accept (any time) |
| No continuous timers to avoid losses | Correct: no timed decay, no escape deadline; the only timed mechanics are trade-offer expiries (player-set, generous) and respect-window cooldowns (self-imposed) |
| Efficient short session | Correct: submit-queued investments + a twinned trade = one 10-minute session ahead of the boundary |
| Two-hour meaning | Correct: effort put in *this* turn is **always** visible by the *next* turn (no multi-turn waits to see a decided action resolve) |

## 15. The finance interface, explanations, and economic legibility (Deliverable 11)

DP-004 ("The world explains itself") and Prompt 6's provenance contract (§9B: every important number traceable to visible causes) bind this deliverable. A player who cannot answer "why did my treasury change?" is playing a spreadsheet blindly.

### 15.1 Distinguishing the six number types (provenance discipline)

Every economic number the UI shows is exactly one of these; the UI labels it, and the backend never sums across types silently:

| Type | Definition | Example | UI treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Current state | Measured balance right now | 4,500 spendable; 200 timber | Plain figure, source of truth |
| 2. Reserved/committed | Held against a submitted order, not yet spent | −300 reserved for a queued mill | Shown next to spendable: "4,200 after commitments" |
| 3. Submitted order | A player request pending boundary or acceptance | Queued investment: 300 | Listed with allow-cancel and its failure policy |
| 4. Forecast | Projection from current state + automatic scheduled effects | "~4,850 at next boundary" | Explicitly conditional, shows its assumptions; never a promise |
| 5. Completed transaction | Resolved event with full accounting | "Trade with Cartha settled: +200 timber / −550" | Immutable log entry |
| 6. Historical record | Retained per SDQ-003's retention policy | Last 30 boundaries' income lines | Collapsed by default, expandable |

Rule: a Forecast (4) must never be rendered with the same visual weight as a Current state (1). This kills the most common legibility failure in competitors: players trusting a projection and then being surprised at the boundary.

### 15.2 National finance view (the one screen that must exist)

Minimum contents, built strictly from the six types:

| Element | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spendable currency | type 1 | |
| Committed amount + list of what for | type 2 + 3 | Each commitment carries its failure policy |
| Materials on hand | type 1 | Each with its major uses visible |
| Incoming income by source (productive, settlements, disclosed event adjustments) | type 4 (per line) | Each line carries the visible cause (line level, emphasis, policy); **no reserve interest exists** (ECON-020) |
| Recurring obligations (upkeep, pensions) | type 3/4 | With per-line breakdown — Prompt 2's "the chore" failure is a single opaque "upkeep" figure |
| Expected boundary change | type 4 | "~+350: income 620, upkeep −270" |
| Projected post-boundary balance | type 4 | With the conditional framing of §14.2 |
| Warnings | derived | "Projected deficit at next boundary unless X" — with X as an actionable cause |
| "Why did my balance change since last turn?" | type 6 | One-click expandable accounting: income lines, sink lines, transfer lines, resize-event lines |

Anti-goal: no charts of historical wealth, no cumulative-wealth counters (AP-008, §12.3), no per-turn net-worth comparison screens.

### 15.3 Investment and development view

Per candidate investment the UI must show: the cost (currency + any material requirement), the expected impact actually caused (a named delta, e.g. "textile line 22 → 26"), the opportunity cost (what else ~this cost buys), any threshold or unlock it interacts with, dependencies (required materials and where they can come from), whether the effect applies immediately or at a boundary, whether it is reversible and at what loss, and which failure policy applies if affordability changes. This is the EMV (§7.1) with its audit strip enabled.

### 15.4 Trade view

Per offer (own or received): the exchange stated as a single sentence ("you give: 200 timber; you get: 550 + 80 wool from Branwen"), totals, current affordability (type 1 vs the pledge), conditional affordability if a queued order may drain funds first (type 3 interaction, per §14.2's no-guarantee rule), counterparty relation state, offer state (open/respect-window/accepted/declined/expired/cancelled), expiry and cancellation controls, and on completion the full record of the settled transaction under the settled-opacity rule (§10.7).

Nudger boundary: the view may set defaults that make honest trades easy (e.g. prefilling a fair-value hint from recent completed trades with the same pair); it may not hide either side's input, misprice, or simulate a reaction to induce acceptance. Defaults are stated, not disguised.

### 15.5 What goes where (the four display surfaces)

| Surface | Contents | Explicitly NOT there |
|---|---|---|
| Finance view (per above) | Everything a managing player needs | No other nations' data, no audit records |
| National digest (SYS-017) | One-screen change since last turn: income/expense causes, boundary results, trade results, any failure policies that triggered | No full ledgers, no historical charts |
| Transaction log (finance detail) | Every completed trade, order result, settlement, resize event with full accounting | Not duplicated into digest beyond one summary line |
| Chronicle (SDQ-003) | Births, succession, crises, notable deals, wars — narrative memory | No numbers-only events; no repeat of digest lines 

Rule of thumb (Prompt 6 provenance, applied): the digest answers "what changed"; the finance view answers "what is true now and next"; the ledger answers "prove it"; the chronicle tells the story. No event is duplicated in more than two surfaces.

### 15.6 Legibility acceptance criteria (testable)

1. A player given a screenshot of the finance view can state, within 60 seconds, (a) spendable, (b) committed, (c) expected change at the next boundary, and (d) the largest current risk.
2. Every digest line has a visible cause chain — no unverifiable aggregation rows.
3. Every forecast is labelled as one, with stated conditions.
4. The "why did it change?" expansion reconstructs the delta exactly (sum of listed causes = actual change) for the observed period.

Status: **Recommended proposal** for the view set; layouts and exact wording belong to the design/production stage.

## 16. Storage, journals, and hosting implications (Deliverable 12; SDQ-003, D-005)

**Status framing:** this is a design-feasibility assessment toward the write-budget question SDQ-003 poses; it is not a backend architecture specification, and it does not finalize D-005 (Cloudflare Workers and D1 remain initial candidates, not a deployment decision).

### 16.1 What the economy must store (record-keeping requirement, provider-neutral)

The economy's durable state is deliberately small. Reconstructing any nation's trustworthy economic position requires exactly:

| Record class | Contents | Growth driver | Write trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current balances | Currency (spendable + reserve), line levels, material stocks | One row per nation (update, not append) | On each mutation |
| Open orders + commitments | Queued investments, offers, reservations | Bounded (cancels/accepts close them); one row per order, rows deleted on closure | On submit/accept/cancel/expiry |
| Completed transaction headers | Trade records (per-pair), investment results, resize events, settlements | One row per completed event | On completion |
| Period summary rows | One row per nation per boundary: income by source, upkeep by line, net | 12 rows/day/nation | One write per nation at boundary |

Not stored as per-event rows: income/upkeep computation inputs (derivable from line levels, emphasis, and policy at read time — derived values are calculated, not recorded); balance *between* mutations (snapshot only); other nations' internal data.

The Prompt 6 insight carries: the protection story needs *who/what/why-event* records for involuntary and settlement events; the economy's own bookkeeping needs only mutation events and per-boundary summaries. Between those two, no third ledger exists.

### 16.2 Event taxonomy and retention (minimum consequential-event set)

| Category | Events | Record form | Retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Durable state change | Balance mutation, line mutation, reserve conversion, offer state change | Mutation row (amount, cause tags, timestamp) | Long-lived (the trust anchor) |
| Player audit trail | Investment submitted/failed, offer made/accepted/declined/cancelled/expired, emphasis change, levy | Event row with actor, cost, failure policy if failed | Long-lived headers; details per SDQ-003 |
| Boundary accounting | Income/expense composition, net | One summary row per nation per boundary | Retained 30 boundaries; then aggregate to monthly roll-up |
| Derived/do-not-store | Forecasts, affordability checks, preview arithmetic, relation slips computed on read | Nothing persisted | n/a |
| Digest/chronicle content | Narrative lines | Per SYS-017 and SDQ-003's own retention question | Per SDQ-003 (open) |
| Integrity signals | Anomaly flags per §13 | Flagged subset of existing rows, no parallel shadow ledger | Per governance stage policy |

### 16.3 Write-budget calculation framework (assumptions explicit, no fabricated population)

With no legitimate population figure (Prompt 4 explicitly declined to invent one), the correct output is a **per-nation, per-day unit budget** that scales linearly, plus the fixed per-boundary world cost:

| Source | Writes per nation per day (12 boundaries) | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Period summary rows | 12 | Fixed per nation |
| Income/upkeep application | 0 extra (folded into summary row update) | — |
| Player-initiated actions | Highly variable | Assume *active* nations ~5–15 mutations/day (orders, trades, emphasis changes) as a **test assumption** for the capacity exercise |
| Trade completions | ~1–3/day per active nation (assumption) | Each completion = dual per-pair record + 2 balance mutations (4 writes nominal) |
| Off-peak nations | ~12 writes/day (summary rows only) | Fixed |

Worked illustration (labeled test assumption, not a forecast): a world of **N** active-equivalent nations under these assumptions generates roughly `N × (12 + ~2×(9±6))` ≈ **N × 30 ± N×12** writes/day at the nominal middle. The exercise's purpose: a provider claim of `W` writes/day supports a world of approximately `N ≈ W / 30` under these assumptions — e.g. any provider with ≥ 100k writes/day headroom supports ~3,000 active-equivalent nations on the economy alone, which exceeds every plausible early-world population by a wide margin. The economy is emphatically not the binding write constraint; the turn engine (SYS-016) and journal/digest retention (SDQ-003) should be tested first with the same framework.

### 16.4 Current infrastructure fact-check (fresh research, dated)

Re-verified 2026-10-22 from official Cloudflare documentation; recorded here because Prompt 1's figures predate and provider limits are explicitly not assumed stable under D-005's own rule:

| Limit | Free-plan value (verified) | Source (accessed 2026-10-22) | Versus Prompt 1's record |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 rows read/day | 5 million | developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/**pricing**/ (billing-metrics table) | Unchanged from Prompt 1; citation re-pointed 2026-10-23 — the *limits* page does not state the billing metrics |
| D1 rows written/day | 100,000 | same pricing page | Unchanged |
| D1 total storage | 5 GB | same pricing page | Unchanged |
| D1 max database size | 500 MB (free) / 10 GB (paid) | developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/ | Unchanged |
| Workers requests/day | 100,000 | developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/ | Unchanged |
| Workers CPU time per request | 10 ms (free) | same | Unchanged |
| **Workers Cron Trigger CPU time** | **10 ms per invocation (free)** | developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/ (page last updated 2026-10-12) | **Prompt 1 recorded 50 ms — DISCREPANCY** |

**The discrepancy matters.** If the boundary resolution must run inside a Cron Trigger invocation on the free plan, 10 ms of CPU may be tight for a busy boundary with per-nation summary writes and trade settlement. Mitigating fact (same source): the 10 ms limit is CPU time, not wall-clock time, and I/O waits (D1 reads/writes) do not consume it — so a boundary that is mostly DB round-trips may fit. **Therefore:** (a) SDQ-003 stays open — resolution is not blockable by *storage* limits, but boundary *CPU* shape now enters the architecture question; (b) this is filed as a technical-architecture risk in the handoff, not resolved here; (c) D-005's provisional status is unchanged — if the boundary does not fit, the answer could equally be a paid tier, batch rescheduling, or a different provider. **Limitation:** page contents verified via research agent on the cited official URLs; no independent secondary verification performed.

No status change justified by economy findings alone.

### 17E. Scenario E — market manipulation attempt

An attempt at the classic genre exploit (Prompt 2's fairness findings; §13's R-14 envelope): a player with three accounts (Maine, Alt-1, Alt-2) floods the (hypothetically mature) market with identical daily Maine↔Alt-1 trades to (a) burn trade-credit between accounts into false relationship records, (b) simulate high relationship standing from zero gameplay, and (c) rehearse a laundering channel for later asset dumps. **What the proposed controls prevent (prevention-first, per the anti-pattern discipline):** fresh-account restrictions (R-13): the two alts cannot trade for their first N days *at all* — the exploit never starts; velocity caps: daily per-pair trade-credit cap makes the volume piece worthless even if identity checks were fooled; trade records being *both-sided and permanent* means any later review has full evidence; trade-credit non-transferability (§13.2's R-13 companion) prevents standing-as-product. **What the controls merely detect:** the *pattern* of two accounts trading only with each other at steady volume is a flag, not a conviction (false-positive discipline: merchants legitimately do repeat business — the §13 response is review, not automatic ban). **Residual damage possible:** one legitimate-looking early partnership between Maine and Alt-1's *pre-restriction window* may have escaped velocity caps — bounded by the fresh-account window itself. **Burden on legitimate players:** none (fee-free directed trades unchanged, no KYC, no approvals); the cost is *only* the N-day young-nation restriction, disclosed in the rules (Prompt 3 §7.5's precedent shape). Status: **Recommended proposal** (the control set), with thresholds deferred to the numbers stage (ECOQ-004 family).

### 17F. Scenario F — boundary-resolution failure (the determinism demo)

Mirano queues an investment at 10:00 (cost 300c + 4 MAT-02, class queued-to-boundary); the reservation model (§10.2) reserves 300c now (available: 320 → 20). At 11:50 it accepts a trade that **pays out** (MAT-02 +6, currency +150) — its affordability improves. At the 12:00 boundary the queued investment resolves first in catalog order (SDQ-018's ordering; the digest shows the queue), spends 300c + 4 MAT-02, succeeds. Contract honored: the preview arithmetic had stated "queued; will resolve at boundary; will fail without reserved funds" — the player saw both sides honestly reserved. **Now the failure case:** instead of the paying trade, at 11:50 an *accepted outgoing* trade drains MAT-02 below the queued order's need: at boundary, the queued investment fails its re-check (§14.2's double-check rule), the reservation for its *currency side* is released intact, the order carries its fixed failure policy (cancel-with-reason; the §10.2 model has neither partial-spend nor silent retry), and the digest line names the cause: "investment at 12:00 cancelled: 2 MAT-02 short after trade with X settled at 11:58" (SDQ-017's provenance bar). No value left the system invisibly: the 300c reservation was never spent; the trade's MAT-02 transfer was legitimate, atomic, and doubly recorded; determinism holds (same queue → same outcome on retry-cleanup, idempotent per §14.3). **Accounting check:** Δc = (+150 received trade) − (0 investment) = +150; ΔMAT-02 = +6 (trade) − 2 (unrelated upkeep) = +4; every unit of it represented in the digest. **Zero partial transfers occur where a ledger would break the C2/C4 symmetry:** this is R-19's zero-partial invariant demonstrated end-to-end.

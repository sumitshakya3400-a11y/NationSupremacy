## 1. How to read these registers

### 1.1 Status discipline

Every requirement row here is a **candidate**. `12A-CR-###` identifiers are allocated in this file only; the global requirement register [03_requirements_traceability.md](../03_requirements_traceability.md) is **unchanged by 12A** and still ends at `REQ-763`. Prompt 12B allocates the global block starting at **`REQ-764`** and converts, merges or rejects each candidate. This mirrors the recorded 07A → 07B, 08A → 08B, 09A → 09B, 10A → 10B and 11A → 11B convention, in which an `A` pass proposes candidates under its own prefix and the `B` pass allocates global identifiers (`DD-193` records the precedent for Prompt 11; `stage-2/03` maintenance rule 5 forbids speculative global IDs).

No candidate is marked `Approved`. Where a candidate restates an **already approved** project policy, its Status cell says `Restates approved policy` and names the decision — the policy is approved, the restatement is not.

### 1.2 Vocabulary

| Field | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Priority | `A` · `B` · `C` | Stage A = required before the First Playable Build; Stage B = required before public launch; Stage C = later hardening. Adopted unchanged from Report 08 §17 staging, so the two registers can be read together. |
| Status | `Restates approved policy` · `New proposal` · `New proposal, closes a registered gap` | Whether the candidate adds anything to the approved corpus |
| Category | the 20 categories of the Prompt 12A brief §15.1 | One per row |
| Owner dependency | `No — 12B decides` · `Yes — owner (UD-nnn)` · `Yes — legal (UD-017)` · `Prompt 14` · `Prompt 13` · `Stage 5/6` | Who must act before the row can become a rule |

### 1.3 Field split

Each requirement carries the fifteen fields required by the brief §15.2. They are split across two linked tables, following the `11A-INT` precedent: **§2** carries identity, statement, category, priority, status, source rule and owner dependency; **§3** carries rationale, evidence, dependencies, security impact, privacy impact, acceptance intent and verification approach. A requirement is incomplete if either of its rows is absent, and the validator says so (`12A-24`).

### 1.4 What these registers deliberately do not contain

No penalty amount, no suspension length, no appeal deadline, no retention day-count not already fixed by `D-026` or `D-042`, no administrator permission table, no database schema, no detection threshold, no numerical confidence formula, and no legal conclusion. Each of those omissions is deliberate: §6 of the brief, §10.3, §12.2, §15.2, §19 and §24 of the Prompt 12A instruction forbid inventing them, and the places where one is genuinely needed are recorded as owner or downstream dependencies (`12A-OPEN-###`, and the new `UD-052`…`UD-056` rows).

## 5. Threat and risk register

### 5.1 Method

Risk is scored on the **ordinal scale already documented in Report 08 §2.2** — likelihood L1–L5 multiplied by impact I1–I5, banded Low 1–6, Medium 8–10, High 12–15, Critical 16–25. Reusing that scale rather than inventing one keeps this register comparable with `TH-001`…`TH-025`; a numeric probability is never asserted, because none is supportable. Each row also carries an evidence **class**, using the five the brief §17 requires:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CONFIRMED` | A weakness identified in the current design or prototype by direct inspection |
| `PLAUSIBLE` | A threat consistent with the design that no control yet fully addresses |
| `RESEARCH` | A risk established by external evidence, transferable with a stated caveat |
| `HYPOTHETICAL` | A scenario not evidenced for this game; retained to prove it was considered |
| `IMPL` | A risk that depends on how a specified control is built, and cannot yet be assessed |

## 6. Requirement quality review and overlap analysis

### 6.1 Review method

Each candidate was tested against the eight quality attributes of the brief §15.3 by rewriting it until it passed: **clarity** (one actor, one obligation), **necessity** (a named threat or a registered gap, or it was deleted), **testability** (a verification approach that could be executed against a document, a schema or a build), **consistency** (no contradiction with an approved decision — every conflict found is listed in §6.3), **atomicity** (split where a row carried two obligations), **traceability** (a source cell that resolves to a real identifier), **feasibility** (buildable within the fixed constraints or explicitly assigned to Stage 5/6), and **dependency accuracy** (`12A-INT` or `12A-CR` cross-reference rather than a bare prose claim).

Twenty-two candidates were rejected during drafting for failing necessity or atomicity, including a proposed universal confidence formula (fails the brief §7.3 constraint), a proposed fixed 12-month appeal deadline (no evidence and no owner authorisation), a proposed permanent device-binding ban-evasion control (rejected by `D-042` and `12A-RS-053`), and twelve rows that only restated an approved decision without adding a testable obligation.

### 6.3 Conflicts found during review, and how each was resolved

These are the substantive findings of this pass, not drafting noise.

1. **Three classification vocabularies for the same fields.** `D-042` uses four handling classes (Public/Private/Restricted/Secret), `TR-021` uses four visibility classes (Public/Alliance/Private/Restricted) and `TR-090` adds record purpose classes, while `WEP-647` states that it "uses B-02's classes" and then enumerates **eight** role levels (`PUBLIC`, `NATION_OWNER`, `PARTICIPANT`, `ALLIANCE_MEMBER`, `ALLIANCE_OFFICER`, `MODERATOR`, `ADMIN`, `INTERNAL`). The claim of inheritance is therefore inaccurate as written. **Not resolved by 12A.** `CR-035` and `CR-036` assign the reconciliation to 12B with the three-axis model, and the discrepancy is recorded as `12A-OPEN-001`.
2. **Two incompatible sanction ladders.** Report 08 §12.3 proposes L0–L5 (six tiers). `NET-038` defines levels 0–6 (seven tiers) with `NET-039` fixing an approved IP-only ceiling at level 1. Both are cited by later documents. **Not resolved by 12A**; `CR-049` requires 12B to publish exactly one ladder, and the choice is owner-adjacent because tier authority is owner-reserved (`UD-053`).
3. **`D-037` versus a declaration register.** The obvious industry answer to household false positives is a declared-shared-access exemption (`12A-RS-044`, `12A-RS-059`). `D-037` explicitly forbids a standing co-resident exemption, and `D-033` already provides an attestation route. **Resolved by design, not by override**: `CR-016` makes a declaration an evidentiary input to review that unlocks nothing, which preserves `D-037` verbatim while capturing the operational benefit.
4. **Erasure versus the permanent spine.** `REQ-024` is Open, `D-026` makes the spine permanent and class-P permanent, while an external erasure right — if a jurisdiction attaches — would reach those rows. **Not resolvable by design**; `CR-046` records the conflict with both duties stated and `UD-055` carries it to the owner and counsel.
5. **Prototype single-writer violation.** `12A-CR-002` contradicts an artefact that exists today (`0003_grants_and_rls.sql:14` granting table-wide UPDATE on a column B-04 owns). The requirement is correct and the prototype is wrong; the prototype is not a security guarantee and 12A neither fixes nor re-tests it. Recorded as the confirmed risk `12A-RK-007`.
6. **`WEP-655` versus a moderation ladder.** B-11 states that it "flags, records and defers" and does not confiscate or publicly shame; any 12B tier that reverses a B-11 reward must therefore act through B-11's own correction hooks (`WPA-36`, `WPA-37`) rather than by writing B-11 state. `CR-056` and `CR-052` make that explicit so the two documents cannot drift.
7. **Alliance appeal boundary versus platform appeals.** `UD-044` reserves the Alliance expulsion and appeal question to the owner. `CR-070`–`CR-074` are scoped to **platform** enforcement only, and `CR-098` forbids creating an Alliance appeal right — the boundary the roadmap registers for Prompt 12.

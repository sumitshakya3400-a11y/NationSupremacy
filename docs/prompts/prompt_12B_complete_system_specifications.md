## 2.1 Mandatory operating behavior

The coding agent must operate autonomously for ordinary project decisions.

Specifically:

1. Do not unnecessarily interrupt the owner for routine design, documentation, technical or organizational decisions.
2. Independently investigate issues using existing project documents, approved decisions, architecture, research evidence and established conventions.
3. Select the most defensible solution consistent with project objectives and existing constraints.
4. Resolve ordinary ambiguities using D-014 and the mandatory autonomous decision policy.
5. Record significant autonomous decisions and their rationale.
6. Update all affected specifications, registers, traceability references and status documents.
7. Use conservative, reversible and architecture-compatible defaults where uncertainty remains.
8. Clearly identify assumptions, provisional parameters and unresolved evidence.
9. Continue all work that can safely proceed when an owner decision is pending.
10. Isolate unresolved dependencies instead of unnecessarily blocking the entire phase.

## 2.2 Mandatory escalation boundaries

Escalate only when a matter genuinely requires:

- Explicit owner authorization.
- Modification or reversal of an approved owner decision.
- Closure of an owner-reserved unresolved decision.
- Legal or regulatory interpretation requiring qualified review.
- A material change to approved game-wide architecture or economic principles.
- A decision outside the authority granted by existing project governance.

Never use autonomous authority to:

- Override approved decisions.
- Close owner-reserved decisions.
- Invent legal conclusions.
- Claim approval that has not been granted.
- Convert provisional recommendations into approved policy.

**Guiding principle: Act autonomously on ordinary matters, escalate exceptional matters, and never exceed delegated authority.**

## 2.3 Autonomous decision records

- Whether owner approval is required.
